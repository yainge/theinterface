What this is: both 44-LED strips wound around one shared coil axis instead of sitting in two separate coils side by side — a literal double helix, previewing ten LED patterns on the real physical mounting shape rather than a flat line. Red-Blue is the only pattern actually implemented in theinterface.ino's LedState today -- it's the sketch's Sparkle Twinkle mechanic, just renamed here now that a second, differently-themed twinkle pattern (Sparkle, see below) also exists. Chase, Rainbow Wave, Lava, Eclipse, Rings, Plant, Slow Rainbow, Sparkle, and Aurora are design previews, not yet wired into the sketch. Lava is a heat-diffusion simulation (cool every LED a little at random, then blend each LED toward the one behind it so heat drifts along the strip, colored through a fixed black → red → orange → yellow → white palette) rather than a moving light -- the heartbeat is the only heat source: each beat pumps a fresh burst of heat in at index 0 (Beat burst), which then flows and cools down the strip like magma with no ambient ignition of its own, so a strip that hasn't beaten in a while cools all the way to black and stays there. Cooling controls how far each burst's flow travels before it dies out, and Flow update throttles how often the heat itself actually changes (independent of the 50fps render loop) so the flow reads as slow-moving lava rather than a fast flicker. Eclipse runs opposite to every other pattern here: the strip idles at full brightness (a cloudy, foggy blue "sun," not a warm one) instead of a dim ambient floor, and each beat is a dimming event rather than a brightening one -- a shadow disc whose center sweeps completely through the strip, index 0 all the way to index LED_COUNT-1, over Travel time, plunging the LEDs it passes over into pure black (Umbra radius) with a pure white ring (Corona width) flashing at the shadow's leading and trailing edge, mimicking the diamond-ring effect at 2nd/3rd contact of a real solar eclipse. That blue itself isn't fixed, though: whenever the two hearts are in sync -- BPM 1 and BPM 2 within Sync threshold of each other, same concept as Plant's flower sync -- the sun slowly shifts to a warm orange-yellow over Sync color transition, then eases back to blue over the same duration once they drift back out of sync. Unlike the shadow (independent per strip, on each participant's own beat timing), this color shift is shared: it's a property of both hearts together, so both strips' suns shift in lockstep rather than each tracking its own sync status. Rings sends one ring of light out from the direction of index 0 on each respective heartbeat, its front sweeping through to index LED_COUNT-1 over Travel time -- unlike Rainbow Wave/Lava's one-sided trailing tail, a ring is a symmetric band (Ring width) centered on its front that fades out on both its leading and trailing edge, the way a ripple would, rather than towing a tail behind it, and it's a fixed pure white on both strips regardless of participant color. Plant is the one pattern where growth is cumulative across beats instead of a transient event that fades back to ambient: each heartbeat adds one grown green LED, starting from index 0 (the "roots") and reaching toward LED_COUNT-1 one beat at a time, rather than firing a pulse. Each freshly-grown LED starts at a bright shimmer and settles down to Mature brightness over Shimmer decay -- brightness doesn't stop there, though: it then keeps fading, slowly and continuously, at Aging decay per frame down to a dim Aged floor that it never fully drops below. Since color here is brightness applied to a hue (not a separate light-over-color layer), dimming is darkening -- the longer ago a leaf was grown, the darker green it reads, with the roots (grown first) visibly darker than the newest growth at the tip by the time a plant finishes growing. Each leaf also picks its own random shade within a yellow-green-to-teal-green range so the stem looks like individual leaves rather than one flat green. Whenever the two hearts are in sync -- BPM 1 and BPM 2 within Sync threshold of each other (default 4 BPM, tighter than DESIGN.md's ~5-10 BPM figure for the real near-sync behavior) -- each beat also blooms Flower count violet flowers at random points along whatever's already grown, excluding the growth tip itself (still busy with its own fresh-growth shimmer). Each bloom fades in over Flower fade-in, holds at full brightness for Flower hold, then eases back out over Flower fade-out -- a temporary tint over the green rather than a permanent recolor, so the stem's own growth/aging keeps going underneath. Once every LED is grown, rather than the next beat cutting the whole strip back to ambient in one abrupt step, the strip eases down to black together over Fade-out -- from wherever each LED's brightness actually was when it finished growing, not a fixed reset -- before a fresh plant starts growing from index 0 again. Slow Rainbow is the one pattern that isn't beat-triggered at all: a continuously flowing rainbow gradient across the whole strip (each LED's hue offset from a shared phase by its position) with a slow brightness "breathing" cycle (Fade period, between Min brightness and Max brightness) that runs whether or not any beats are firing. Hue speed is how fast the colors drift at Reference BPM -- the actual speed scales with the live average of BPM 1 and BPM 2, so both hearts moving faster together visibly speeds up how quickly the colors move, and slower hearts drift more languidly; that speed is shared across both strips (like Eclipse's sync color) since it's a property of both participants together, not either one alone. Heartbeats don't flash brightness here like they do almost everywhere else in this file -- instead, each beat gives the drifting colors a brief extra push (Beat kick), reusing the same beatPulse timer every pattern gets from triggerBeat() as the kick's own decay curve, so a beat reads as the rainbow's colors visibly jumping/sliding forward for a moment rather than the strip flashing, easing back to the normal BPM-driven pace as beatPulse decays. It's still ambient mood lighting that never stops or resets for a beat, just one where the heartbeat moves the colors instead of the brightness. Sparkle is a second, differently-themed twinkle alongside Red-Blue: warm yellow-gold (Twinkle peak) instead of red/blue, and not themed per strip -- both strips share the same hue range. Heart rate drives it two ways: each strip's ambient pop rate responds continuously to heart rate, scaling Min interval/Max interval (in frames, at Reference BPM) by that strip's own current BPM -- a faster heart makes its own strip twinkle faster, a slower one twinkles more languidly, independently per participant rather than a shared/combined value like Eclipse or Slow Rainbow use -- and on top of that, each heartbeat directly lights Beat sparkle count extra sparkles at Beat sparkle brightness, brighter than the ambient Twinkle peak so the beat's own moment stands out against the background twinkling rather than blending into it. Aurora mimics the aurora borealis as an organic, continuously animated "curtain" of light -- three overlapping sine waves at different spatial frequencies and phase speeds (Wave speed) combine so the bright bands and gaps between them never look like one clean repeating wave, and only the combined waves' positive lobes glow (Base brightness is the floor in the gaps between bands). Green-to-teal dominates, the most common real aurora color, but position along the strip layers in the other colors real auroras show at their fringes: index 0 (the bottom, same convention Plant uses) shifts to blue and purple over roughly the lowest third of the strip, and the very top shifts to a red fringe over roughly the top 15% -- rarer and higher-altitude in a real aurora, so it only gets a small stretch of LEDs here, not a washed-out tint everywhere. Like Sparkle, each strip's own current BPM independently scales *its own* curtain's intensity (Intensity reference BPM) -- a faster heart brightens its aurora, a slower one dims it. On top of all that, whenever the two hearts are in sync (Sync threshold, same concept as Plant/Eclipse) a rare magenta-pink shimmer -- real auroras do show pink/red during intense geomagnetic activity -- fades in over Sync color transition wherever the curtain is already brightest, and eases back to the ordinary coloring once they drift apart; that shimmer is shared across both strips, a property of both hearts together rather than either one alone. The selected pattern applies to both participants at once; each still runs its own independent beat timing, sparkle positions, wave state, heat array, eclipse state, ring state, plant growth/flowers/fade-out, rainbow phase/breathing, twinkle state, and aurora phase/intensity, same as the two real HeartChannels. Only Chase (plain, fixed red on both strips) still reads LED color 1 / LED color 2 to tell participants apart by color; every other pattern owns its own fixed color treatment instead (red-to-orange & blue-to-purple themes / hue sweep / heat palette / cloudy blue / pure white / leafy greens / warm yellow-gold / green-blue-purple-red aurora), same as the real BEAT_R/BEAT_G/BEAT_B constants are a single shared color in theinterface.ino today. Sparkle stays tellable apart by theme rather than one flat color: Strip 1 sparkles are a constantly changing random assortment of red-to-orange, Strip 2 a changing assortment of blue-to-purple -- each lit LED (ambient twinkle or beat sparkle) rolls its own random hue within its strip's range every time it's freshly lit, rather than all sharing one color. Rainbow Wave moves the same direction on both -- index 0 to index LED_COUNT-1 -- and unlike Eclipse's shadow, multiple waves can be traveling at once: every beat launches its own, so beats landing close together (fast BPM, or the two participants' own beats) produce overlapping waves rather than one beat's wave cutting off another's, each still always completing its own full sweep before dropping out. So Strip 2's mirrored placement (below) is the only thing that makes any of it look like it's coming from the other side. Strip 2 winds from the opposite end of the coil to Strip 1 -- its index 0 sits at the top instead of the bottom -- so their placement mirrors each other; the underlying data isn't touched, index 0 is still each participant's own first LED. With Double helix unchecked, the two strips are simply 180° apart (P1, P2) around the coil. Checked, each participant additionally draws a second strand of their own 180° from their first, and the two participants are spaced 90° apart instead, so all four strands (P1a/P1b/P2a/P2b) land evenly around the coil without overlapping — the second strand of each pair renders the same participant's one LED array (fuller-looking coil, no gaps when viewed edge-on), not a second data source or a second physical strip. Each participant's non-primary strand is drawn dimmer and its index labels are hidden so the four strands stay readable. Red-Blue's brightness/decay math -- Ambient floor / Sparkle peak (LED_AMBIENT_FLOOR, LED_SPARKLE_PEAK) and the per-pixel decay curve -- still matches theinterface.ino exactly, but two things here are design changes, not yet ported back: (1) each lit LED's color is now a random hue drawn from that strip's theme (see above) rather than theinterface.ino's single flat BEAT_R/BEAT_G/BEAT_B color, and (2) on-beat behavior -- instead of flooding every LED to full brightness (what LedState::checkBeat() still does in the sketch today), each beat here lights a random assortment of Beat sparkles LEDs (default 14 of 44), which then fade out individually through the same per-pixel decay ambient twinkles use -- Beat decay (LED_BEAT_DECAY) no longer shapes the flash itself, it now just times how long ambient twinkles pause after a beat so they don't compete visually with the beat's own sparkles. Turns/radius/height are just this preview's placeholders for the actual physical coil dimensions — plug in real measurements once the mounting is finalized.
